    How a breadboard works

    • The long lines at the top and bottom are the power rails. Every hole on the red + line is joined, and every hole on the blue/black − line is joined.
    • In the middle, each column of 5 holes (a to e, or f to j) is joined. The gap in the middle splits them.
    • So if the ESP32 pin is in hole 14c, a wire in hole 14a touches that pin.
    • Some breadboards cut the rails in half in the middle. If yours has a gap in the red/blue line, bridge the two halves with a short jumper.

    Reading your resistors

    • 220 Ω (for the LEDs): red, red, brown, gold. On 5-band resistors: red, red, black, black, brown.
    • 10 kΩ: brown, black, orange, gold. Not used in this build; keep as spares.
    • Not sure? Multimeter on Ω mode: a 220 Ω resistor reads about 215–225.

    Legs that have a direction

    • LED: long leg is + (goes to the ESP32 side), short leg is − (flat edge on the rim).
    • 100 µF capacitor: long leg is +. The stripe with "−" marks goes on the − rail.
    • Resistors, the small 104 capacitor and the push button work either way round.

    Test 1: does the radio answer?

    
    
    Open Serial Monitor at 115200. Radio OK means your 8 radio wires are right. Radio error: -2 means the ESP32 can't find the radio: check NSS, SCK, MOSI, MISO, RST.
    
  
    
  
    
    
    Test 2: HELLO / ACK between two nodes
    
    
    
    
    Upload with NODE_NAME "A" on the first node and "B" on the second. Press the button (or type in Serial Monitor and press Enter). The other node's screen shows the message with RSSI and SNR, its red LED blinks, and it sends an ACK back.
